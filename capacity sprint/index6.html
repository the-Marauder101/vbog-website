01 / Why this happens to good agencies

You can add one more client. You can't add ten.

One more client fits in the gaps. Ten needs a person. And people don't come in halves — you can't buy 30% of a strategist.

So growth stops being a slope and becomes a jump. And the jump is expensive, upfront, and paid for out of profit you can see.

01

The salary starts before the revenue does.

A decent manager begins at ₹70–80K. Someone who can hold strategy across thirty accounts costs more. You pay from month one. The clients that justify it show up over six.

02

So you do that job for free.

Strategy, review, the difficult client call — all of it routes to you, because you're already paid for. Cheapest possible answer. Also the reason nothing moves.

03

Your team executes. Nobody decides.

They can do the work. They can't judge it. So every unclear thing comes back up to you — and hiring more juniors adds output and adds review in the same breath.

04

You choose the profit. Every time.

Hold the book, protect the margin, put your energy somewhere the numbers work. It's the right call on the information you have. It's also a ceiling that never lifts.

08 / Book only if this is true

Don't book if you need more leads.

We run a small number of these at once, because we are inside the business for all ninety days. This is the same filter I use on the call — save yourself the thirty minutes if the right column is you.

Strong fit

  • ₹12L+ monthly agency revenue, ₹75K+ average client value
  • 5–12 people, most of them executing rather than deciding
  • Real demand — referrals or pipeline you haven't moved on
  • You personally do the strategy, the reviews, or both
  • You've priced a senior hire and decided against it
  • You'll give someone on your team real authority

Do not book

  • You need more leads, not more capacity
  • Your service is not yet proven or repeatable
  • You want a revenue or profit guarantee
  • You want documents or software, not change
  • You intend to keep making every decision
  • Your team will not participate in the change

09 / Who does the work

I lead it. My team runs it.

I own the diagnosis, the design and every decision that matters — and I'm on your calls every week. My team does the building, the documentation and the day-to-day implementation alongside your people.

"I'm selling you a business that doesn't depend on one person. It would be strange to run mine that way."

That's deliberate. An agency that stalls because everything routes through the founder is exactly the problem we fix — so VBOG is built not to have it. You get senior judgement without waiting in a queue for it.

What we don't do: sales, media buying, creative, or your pipeline. If leads are your real constraint, I'll say so on the call and point you somewhere more useful.

Will this double my revenue?

No, and I won't pretend otherwise. We build the capacity and the ownership so your agency can take on more work without adding ₹5L a month of people. Whether that capacity fills depends on your pipeline, your pricing and your close rate — none of which I control.

Will my people really go from 5 accounts to 13?

That's what happened in one engagement with one service mix. It is not a promise. Your number depends on how complex your accounts are and how much judgement each piece of work needs. The first two weeks establish where you are now and where you can credibly get to — and if the answer is "barely further than today," I'll tell you the Sprint isn't worth ₹4L to you.

Who ends up running this after you leave?

Usually a senior person already on your team who has judgement but has never been given authority. Finding that person is part of the first two weeks. If genuinely nobody fits, we write the role and help you hire for it — but at a junior salary, because the system already exists by then. That's the difference from hiring a ₹1L manager into a vacuum.

Why not just hire the ops manager?

You still might, later. But a manager who joins an agency with no capacity number, no written decision rules and no single view of the work spends six months learning the business while firefighting, and gets bypassed the first time something is urgent. This is sequencing, not substitution.

What exactly is guaranteed?

That the agreed system is built and in live use by Day 90 — assuming you participate, give access, and let decisions move. If it isn't live, I keep working at no extra fee until it is. Revenue, margin and profit are not guaranteed by me or by anyone honest.

Do you take equity or a share of profit?

No. Fixed fee, fixed scope, one engagement, then it's yours.

What if my team resists it?

Some resistance is normal and we plan for it. Sustained resistance from you is fatal — if you keep making the decisions you've just handed over, nothing holds. That's the single biggest reason this fails, and it's why the fit filter above is worded the way it is.
